# A checked delivery for every audience

**Target user:** a B2B webinar or product-demo producer who reuses prepared recordings and a companion slide deck for sales, customer education, and events. The useful starting point is a modular presentation: complete recorded sections, matching slides, and an explicit list of content that must travel together.

**Job:** “Turn this approved material into the version this audience needs, within its time limit. Give me the matching video and deck, ready to review together. If a revision cannot satisfy the requirements, keep the working delivery.”

An executive introduction might favor the problem and result; a customer walkthrough might favor the workflow. Both could have the same time limit while needing different content. These are proposed use cases, not reports from customers. HardStop’s unit of delivery is the video, copied deck, and unsent handoff draft generated from one brief.

## Where economic value could come from

Each audience version creates repeated work: interpreting the brief, selecting recordings, trimming the companion deck, checking duration and required context, saving the outputs, and preparing the handoff. A later change can require those steps again. HardStop automates and checks this sequence across Gmail, Dropbox, and Google Slides.

The value hypothesis is reduced manual coordination and fewer corrections after delivery. Source preparation, human review, API charges, and rendering still have costs. There are no measured time savings, customer interviews, paid users, or revenue claims behind this submission. An economic evaluation should compare the same revisions completed manually and with HardStop, counting total effort, corrections, and runtime cost—including preparation and review.

## What has been demonstrated

The original public demonstration uses eight fictional recordings with synthesized narration. Its live evidence includes successful 120- and 90-second briefs, a custom 75-second brief, a correctly blocked 30-second request, and a controlled stale-input test. The checks cover explicit requirements and declared dependencies. They do not establish that every nuance of an arbitrary talk has been preserved. [Read the evidence and its limits](EVIDENCE.md).

The same 90-second cap and required closure also produced two completed three-app audience versions: **85.603 seconds for the buyer**, with the problem explanation, and **86.203 seconds for the operator**, with the workflow explanation. Both passed 25 recorded checks. A separate predeclared six-case model evaluation passed all six first attempts, including supported paraphrases and two review outcomes. These are authored demonstrations on fictional material, not customer validation. [Conditions and complete evaluation](EVIDENCE.md#live-audience-versions).

The [source importer](SOURCE_GUIDE.md) supports a new package of 1–18 prepared recordings, declared transcripts and slide text in a separate workspace. The independent fictional Harbor package completed that path at 43.601 seconds, with its initial review outcome and later clarified brief both recorded. It generates the source Slides deck from supplied text; it does not import an arbitrary existing deck or prepare the producer's recordings automatically. Support for this format is separate from proof that an outside producer can prepare it easily or prefers this workflow. The next demand test is whether a producer with existing modular material can create the needed variants and review the complete deliveries with less total effort.
